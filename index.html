Careless Mistakes in Maths

What They Really Are — And How to Reduce Them
Understanding the Problem
Is It Actually Careless?

Before blaming carelessness, ask: if I redid this right now, would I get it right?

3 things mistaken for careless:

  • Concept not fully understood — it's a knowledge gap
  • Not enough repetition — doubt creeps in when a skill isn't automatic yet
  • Careless as a convenient excuse — protects the ego, but stops you from improving
What Causes It
Fatigue
  • Brain's working memory weakens — mistakes you'd normally catch slip through

What to do:

  • 7–8 hrs sleep the night before
  • Light meal before exam
  • Final hour: review formulas and key concepts only — avoid attempting hard questions
Complacency
  • Autopilot on familiar questions — rushing past key details
  • x² = 9 → only writing x = 3, forgetting x = −3
  • Forgetting +c in indefinite integrals

What to do:

  • Don't rush — maintain a steady pace even on questions that feel familiar
  • Read the full question before writing anything
Types of Mistakes
Reading Errors
  • Missed 'hence', 'exact form', or misread a sign

What to do:

  • Underline key instruction words before starting
  • Do not rush, even on easy questions
Process Errors
  • Right method, wrong execution — sign flip, wrong expansion, forgot chain rule coefficient

What to do:

  • Write every step out
  • Minimise skipping lines, even when the step feels obvious
How to Check Your Work
General Checking Methods
  • Substitute back — does your answer satisfy the original equation?
  • Sense-check magnitude — negative length? Probability greater than 1? Something went wrong upstream.
  • Re-read the question — not your working. Did you answer what was actually asked?
Keith's Take — When to Check
  • Confident questions — finish paper first, check at the end
  • Long / harder questions — quick check after each key step. Does this intermediate value look reasonable?
Building the Habit for Checking
Time Allocation Guide

Checking must be within the time limit — not added on after.

Total marks × 1.5 = minutes
  • 20 marks → 30 minutes
  • 30 marks → 45 minutes

Doesn't have to be a full paper — 30 or 45 min sessions count.

Track — Where Mistakes Happen

Note which topics or question types you make mistakes in.

This signals where you need more practice — not just more checking.

Track — What Kind of Mistakes

Missing 'hence' or 'exact form'? Wrong decimal places or significant figures?

Identify the pattern. These become your personal watchlist for the actual exam.

Build the habits. Reduce the mistakes — such that on your best day, they are almost zero.
